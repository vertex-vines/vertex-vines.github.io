Collaborative Research: VINES: Track 1: Quantum-Ready and AI-Enabled by Design: Resilient and Deployable Next-Gen Cellular Networks

Summary

Next-generation (NextG) wireless networks will support smart cities, autonomous vehicles, and immersive communication, so they must keep performing under failures, attacks, and disruptions while protecting sensitive data and user privacy. Deployments are growing fast and base stations and servers are increasingly exposed to remote and physical access, so protocols must support distributed trust and stay secure even when parts of the network are breached. At the same time, quantum computers threaten today's cryptographic foundations, so protocols must be post-quantum (PQ) secure to remain safe long term.

This project aims to create, develop, and evaluate a vertically integrated framework for resilient and PQ-secure NextG networks. 1) New compact, compromise-resilient, and privacy-preserving PQ signature schemes (identity-based, threshold, and blind signatures) will be designed for constrained NextG environments. 2) New resilient and PQ-secure cellular protocols will be developed for initial bootstrapping, authentication and key agreement (5G-AKA, AKMA), and MVNO–MNO deployments, and formally verified. 3) Trustworthy AI for NextG will be developed, including federated-learning-based intrusion detection, PQ-secure federated learning with adaptive differential privacy, and verifiable model integrity. All results will be evaluated end to end on RIPPLE (Resilient Intelligent Protocol Platform for Large-scale Evaluation), an open evaluation fabric built on the NSF Colosseum and POWDER testbeds.
